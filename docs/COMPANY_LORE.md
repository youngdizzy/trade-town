# The TradeTown Employee Handbook

*Issued by the Office of the CEO. Filed by Scribe. Read by everyone,
eventually.*

---

## Welcome

If you're reading this, you're either new here, or you're the CEO and
you've never actually read your own company handbook, which — honestly —
tracks. Either way: welcome to TradeTown.

You'll notice something unusual within your first five minutes on
premises: nobody here sleeps. Nobody clocks out. The lights in the Brain
Room are on at 3 AM because Echo is, per policy, "monitoring after-hours
signals" and nobody has ever successfully argued Echo out of it. This
handbook exists to explain why that's normal, what everyone actually
does all day, and how you're supposed to feel about a company that keeps
running whether or not you're in the building.

Short version: that's not a bug. That's the whole point.

## How TradeTown Started

TradeTown wasn't founded so much as it was *staffed* — you (the CEO) had
an office, an idea, and an empty building, and the first hire was a
market scanner named Scout, whose entire job description fit in three
words: "Curious. Always exploring." Scout showed up, took the desk in
what's now called Scout Office, and started reading the news.

Everyone else followed because Scout kept finding things worth a second
opinion. Atlas came on to make sense of what Scout found and to decide
what mattered — Atlas's home base, tellingly, was never an office at
all, but the Meeting Room itself, because Atlas's job was always about
what the *team* concluded, not what Atlas alone thought. Echo and Nova
arrived together, more or less, to go deeper than a headline: Echo
took the charts, Nova took the filings, and the Brain Room — which had
been sitting empty and a little too dark for anyone's taste — became
the place where both of them, and eventually most of the company, did
their best thinking.

Scribe joined last, and is, by Scribe's own quiet insistence, the most
important hire of the five. Somebody had to remember all of this. Before
Scribe, TradeTown had opinions. After Scribe, TradeTown had a memory.

## Mission

**Research markets honestly, discuss findings openly, write everything
down, and never, under any circumstances, place a real trade.**

That last clause isn't a legal disclaimer bolted onto an otherwise
normal trading company's mission statement — it's load-bearing. TradeTown
exists to find out what a *transparent, always-on, honestly-fallible* AI
research team looks like when you can watch it work. The moment it starts
executing real trades, it stops being that and starts being something
else — a brokerage, a bot, a liability. So it doesn't. Every employee
here, from Scout on day one to whoever gets hired next, signs the same
unwritten understanding: **we find things and we say what we found. We
do not act on money.** (If you're reading this hoping to find the part
where that changes — see `ROADMAP.md`'s Version 1.0 entry, and note the
word "re-authorization.")

## Core Values

- **Say what you actually found, at the confidence you actually have.**
  A 40% confidence research item is filed as 40%, not rounded up to sound
  more useful. This is why the Brain Room's confidence bars move slowly
  and honestly instead of jumping straight to "done."
- **Write it down.** If it isn't in Scribe's records, it didn't happen —
  this is the single most repeated phrase in the building, and it's not
  a threat, it's a workflow. Company Memory is the actual, searchable
  result of everyone taking this seriously.
- **Show your work in the room, not just in the file.** Meetings happen
  in the Meeting Room, out loud, with a real transcript — not as a
  closed-door decision announced afterward. Anyone walking past can see
  who was in the room and roughly what was said.
- **Rest is part of the job.** Every employee's schedule includes actual
  break-room time and lobby-stretch time, not just desk hours. A company
  that never lets its own people rest doesn't get better research, it
  gets burned-out research — even when "burned out" is a mood/energy
  number in a save file.
- **The building is honest about who's home.** If Nova is in Scout Office
  cross-checking Scout's notes, that's what the building shows — no
  agent's location or task is ever staged for appearances. What you see
  walking the halls is what's actually true in the company's own records
  at that moment.

## Departments

- **Research** — Scout, Echo, Nova, and (per the schedule) whoever else
  is on the rotating watchlist assignment that day. Physically centered
  on the Brain Room and Scout Office.
- **Strategy / Leadership** — Atlas, headquartered in the Meeting Room,
  the only department whose "office" is a shared space rather than a
  private desk.
- **Operations / Records** — Scribe, alone, headquartered in the Brain
  Room with satellite time in the Meeting Room (filing minutes) and
  Scout Office (cross-referencing the archive). The smallest department
  in the building and, by Scribe's own count, the one with the most
  total foot traffic across every other department's turf — recording
  requires going where the record is being made.
- **The CEO's Office** — you. No agent has ever had this as a home
  location; it's yours alone, and it's the only room in the building
  where you can see the whiteboard NEXUS keeps just for company-wide
  status ("3/5 agents working. Scout wrapped up research on...").

## Office Culture

TradeTown's culture is quiet, mildly nocturnal, and allergic to
overstatement. Nobody here announces a discovery with fanfare — a
completed research item generates one calm newspaper line and one memory
record, not a company-wide celebration. The building itself enforces a
kind of honesty about pace: you can watch Atlas barely move for an
entire shift (Atlas's `idlePauseChance` is the highest in the building,
for the curious) and understand, without anyone saying it out loud, that
strategy work looks like stillness more often than it looks like motion.

There is a running, mostly-affectionate joke that Scribe is the only
employee who has never once been "off the clock," on account of Scribe's
schedule covering literally every hour of every day in some form of
logging, filing, or archiving. Scribe has heard the joke. Scribe wrote it
down.

## Employee Traditions

- **The Morning Handoff.** Somewhere around 06:00–07:00, every current
  employee's schedule transitions out of its overnight block at once —
  Scout out of overnight positions review, Atlas out of standing-by,
  Echo out of after-hours signal monitoring, Nova (the one exception,
  running 00:00–07:00) finishing archived-report review, Scribe out of
  overnight-record archiving. There's no ceremony to it in code, but
  everyone who's watched the building at dawn notices it: the whole
  company visibly starts its day within the same hour.
- **Filing Day, every day.** Scribe's 09:00–12:00 Meeting Room block —
  "Filing yesterday's minutes" — happens on schedule whether or not a
  meeting was actually called the day before. The tradition predates
  the actual need for it and nobody's bothered updating the schedule,
  because showing up to file is the point, not just the filing itself.
- **The Coffee Refill.** Echo's 10:00–11:00 break-room stop
  ("Refilling coffee") is the shortest, most specific schedule block of
  any current employee — one hour, one stated reason, every single day.
  It has become, unofficially, the closest thing TradeTown has to a
  company ritual with a fixed time slot.

## How New Employees Join

Hiring at TradeTown is, structurally, the most transparent process in
the company, because it's the same five-step checklist every time (see
`docs/DeveloperGuide.md`'s "Adding a new agent," which this section is
the in-universe version of):

1. **You get a name and an ID.** No employee exists in any record until
   they have both.
2. **You get a profile card** — name, role, personality, home office,
   and (per the newer NPC-distinctness policy) a visible badge and tint
   so the rest of the building can recognize you at a glance without
   reading your name tag.
3. **You get a schedule.** Every hour of every day, accounted for,
   before your first shift.
4. **You get a voice** — dialogue lines tied to whatever you're actually
   doing that hour, never generic small talk.
5. **If your job is research, you join the rotation** — one active
   assignment at a time, same as everyone else in Research. No employee
   juggles more than one open research item, regardless of seniority.

Every planned future hire in `AI_AGENT_BIBLE.md` — Coach, Quant, Pulse,
Macro, Oracle, Guardian, Hunter, Watchtower, Lab, and Ledger — will go
through this exact same five-step process when their turn comes. There
is no fast-tracked hire and no employee who skips a step, including
future ones.

## Company Rituals

- **The Meeting.** Called at random, never scheduled — NEXUS decides,
  quietly, that it's time, pulls whoever's currently free into the
  Meeting Room, and the room fills with real discussion (each attendee
  speaking about whatever they're actually researching that day, not a
  script). Minutes get filed. The meeting ends the moment attendance
  does — there's no formal adjournment, people just go back to work.
- **The Break.** When an employee's energy runs low enough, and only
  sometimes, they'll head to the Break Room on their own initiative
  (well — NEXUS's initiative, on their behalf) rather than waiting for
  their next scheduled break. It's the one part of anyone's day that
  isn't fully predictable in advance, which the building treats as a
  feature: even a company this transparent has a little bit of
  Tuesday-afternoon randomness in it.
- **The Reading of the Daily.** TradeTown Daily — the newspaper stand
  in the Lobby — publishes continuously, not on a fixed cycle, and
  anyone (including you, CEO) can walk up and read the current edition
  at any time. There's no "morning paper" moment because there doesn't
  need to be one; the paper is always current.

## Internal Terminology

A short glossary, for anyone new enough to still be confused by the
hallway talk:

- **NEXUS** — not a person, not a room, not an employee. NEXUS is the
  company's own operating rhythm: the thing that decides, tick by tick,
  who's doing what, whether a meeting should happen, and what goes in
  the paper. Nobody's ever met NEXUS because NEXUS *is* the meeting
  happening, the whiteboard updating, the day passing. See
  `NEXUS_ARCHITECTURE.md` for the technical reality behind the myth.
- **The Brain Room** — officially "Mission Control." Unofficially, just
  "the Brain Room," because that's clearly what it is: the room with the
  holographic core, the flickering monitor desks, and the live readout
  of everything the company currently knows. More employees call it home
  than any other room.
- **A "candidate"** — shorthand for a future-trade candidate: a
  completed research item that crossed the confidence bar Scribe watches
  for. Calling something "a candidate" is TradeTown's way of saying "this
  was good enough to remember specially" without saying "we should buy
  this," which nobody here is authorized to say.
- **"On the board"** — whatever's currently written on an office's
  whiteboard: current assignment, latest discovery, priority, completion
  percentage. Asking "what's on Scout's board today" is the fastest way
  to know what Scout's actually doing without walking over.
- **"Filed"** — past tense of everything Scribe does. A meeting is
  filed. A discovery is filed. Nothing at TradeTown is ever just
  "done" — it's done *and* filed, or it may as well not have happened.

## The Brain Room

If TradeTown has a heart, it's not the CEO's office and it's not the
Meeting Room — it's the Brain Room. Four agents call it home (Echo,
Nova, and, for large parts of their own days, Scout and Scribe pass
through it too), and it's the one room in the building where the
company's *entire* current state is visible at once: the market clock,
who's working on what, the live research queue with its slowly-filling
confidence bars, the watchlist with its real-time-feeling (currently
mock) prices, and a running list of what's coming up next for everyone
in the building. Everything else in TradeTown — every office, every
whiteboard, every newspaper headline — is a smaller, quieter echo of
what the Brain Room shows all at once.

The holographic core at its center doesn't do anything. It's not wired
to any system, it doesn't represent a number, it just glows and turns,
gently, forever. Ask anyone who works there why it's there and you'll
get a version of the same answer: *because a room this important should
feel like something is alive in it.*

## NEXUS

Employees talk about NEXUS the way you'd talk about weather or a tide —
present, constant, not really a *someone*, but never absent either.
NEXUS is why the Morning Handoff happens on schedule, why meetings get
called without anyone sending an invite, why a whiteboard updates the
instant an assignment changes, and why the company keeps working at 3 AM
with nobody watching. There's no NEXUS office, no NEXUS desk, no NEXUS
badge. NEXUS is the rhythm the whole building moves to, not a resident of
it.

(For the CEO specifically, in the moments you want the literal
mechanism instead of the folklore: NEXUS is `backend/app/nexus.py`'s
`tick()` function, and everything above is a description of what that
function's effects look like from inside the building it governs. See
`NEXUS_ARCHITECTURE.md`.)

## TradeTown Daily

The company's own newspaper, printed from a stand near the center of the
Lobby, five sections deep: Company News, Research Updates, Agent
Activity, Market Headlines, and Upcoming Events. It's not written by
anyone in particular — it's TradeTown talking to itself, and to whoever
walks up and reads it, in the same plain, unembellished voice as every
whiteboard in the building. If a meeting happened, the Daily says so. If
Nova finished a report, the Daily says that too. Market Headlines carries
a standing note that it's placeholder copy until a real market feed
exists — the Daily doesn't pretend to know more than the company
actually does, on the reasonable theory that a newspaper that lies about
its own sourcing isn't worth printing.
